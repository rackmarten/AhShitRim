# AhShitRim: design

> Status: milestone 1 done. San Andreas runs hidden as a simulator of CJ, driven from Skyrim with F10, on Skyrim's own terrain and statics, built around him as collision objects (protocol v4). Skyrim draws CJ from San Andreas's own model, animated by San Andreas, through San Andreas's camera (protocol v5). CJ spawns, drives and rides San Andreas's vehicles there, which Skyrim draws from their own models too (protocol v6), checked in game from Whiterun to Riverwood and past two floating origin moves. While CJ has the player, Skyrim draws San Andreas's HUD and text styles in San Andreas's own fonts, and San Andreas plays its own sound and radio at Skyrim's volumes, with Radio Sovngarde on Skyrim's own music (protocol v7). E activates what CJ faces through Skyrim, and he stays the player through load doors, interiors and fast travel, re-anchored where the game puts the player (protocol v8). He faces whoever talks to him, shouts with Skyrim's own Shout key, and follows Skyrim's player through scenes and seats that take it over (protocol v9), and Sweetroll FM on CC0 music that ships with AhShitRim (protocol v10). Skyrim's health is the only health: the damage San Andreas works out for CJ is taken off the player (armour first), dying is Wasted, and CJ survives saving and loading (protocol v11). He carries San Andreas's weapons: the wheel switches them, the right mouse button aims and the left one fires, each weapon is drawn in his hand from its own model with a muzzle flash, its icon and ammo are in San Andreas's HUD and the loadout is in the save (protocol v12). His guns and blows hurt Skyrim's actors: each shot is hit tested in Skyrim, headshots count, damage scales with the target's level and size, the player gets the kill and the crime, and his weapon skills grow and are saved (protocol v13). San Andreas's cheat codes are typed in Skyrim, with NOMERCY of our own (protocol v20). San Andreas's wanted stars ride on Skyrim's bounties, with mounted guards and Busted (protocol v22). Skyrim's quests start and end as San Andreas's missions, with its mission passed tune, and a new game opens with CJ's "Ah shit, here we go again." in the Helgen cart (protocol v24).

AhShitRim runs **GTA: San Andreas** and **Skyrim Anniversary Edition** at the same time. Skyrim owns the world and draws everything; San Andreas runs hidden and simulates CJ, his cars, his guns and his cheat codes.

It's a sibling of [PerkeleRim](https://github.com/rackmarten/PerkeleRim) (My Summer Car in Skyrim) and is built the same way. Where this document is silent, PerkeleRim's `docs/DESIGN.md` describes the shared approach.

## 1. Goals

- CJ on foot and in San Andreas's vehicles, with San Andreas's own movement, animations and vehicle physics, on Skyrim's terrain and statics.
- Skyrim keeps working as Skyrim: its own player actor stays the real player, so dialogue, quests, bounties and kill credit keep working. CJ is the body the player drives.
- No assets from either game in the repository or a release. Models, textures and sounds are read from the player's own installs at play time. The only audio that ships is Sweetroll FM's CC0 music (`music/CREDITS.md`).

## 2. Non-goals (for now)

- Quests of our own (Skyrim's are shown as San Andreas's missions, see Missions; after milestone 1, see the README). Of the cheat codes, the ped, traffic, gang, weather and time ones, and the wanted level ones that only set the stars. San Andreas's police scanner.
- San Andreas's own world. Its map is emptied; only Skyrim's world exists.
- Any San Andreas exe other than 1.0 US, and the Definitive Edition (a different engine).

## 3. Architecture

```
 Skyrim AE (64-bit)                          GTA: San Andreas 1.0 US (32-bit, hidden)
 ┌──────────────────────────────┐            ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ SKSE plugin  (skse/)         │            │ ASI plugin  (sa/)                  │
 │ CommonLibSSE-NG, C++         │  shared    │ plugin-sdk, C++                    │
 │ - samples Skyrim collision   │  memory    │ - empties SA's map                 │
 │ - forwards input             │ ◄────────► │ - builds Skyrim's ground around CJ │
 │ - draws CJ and the car       │ protocol/  │ - runs CJ and vehicle physics      │
 │ - owns quests, NPCs, saves   │            │ - streams poses and events back    │
 └──────────────────────────────┘            └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Skyrim side** (`skse/`): an SKSE plugin on CommonLibSSE-NG, the same build setup as PerkeleRim's `skse/`. Code from PerkeleRim is copied in as each increment needs it (collision sampler, model building, input forwarding, launcher pieces); there is no shared library yet.
- **San Andreas side** (`sa/`): a 32-bit ASI plugin on [plugin-sdk](https://github.com/DK22Pac/plugin-sdk), loaded by an ASI loader. [gta-reversed](https://github.com/gta-reversed/gta-reversed) is the reference for San Andreas's internals.
- **Link** (`protocol/`): one versioned shared memory section; see [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md). The same C++ link code compiles into both plugins (64-bit and 32-bit), so the header uses fixed-width types only.

### One exe version

Only GTA: San Andreas **1.0 US** is supported: one exe means one set of memory addresses, and plugin-sdk and gta-reversed both target it. The current Steam build is downgraded to it once (docs/DEV_SETUP.md). On any other exe the ASI logs why and does nothing.

### Ownership rules

- Skyrim owns the world, the player actor, NPCs, quests, crime and saves.
- San Andreas owns CJ's and the vehicles' physics and animation state while the player is handed to it (F10, as in PerkeleRim).
- Each shared memory slot has exactly one writer.

### Ground: Skyrim's collision in San Andreas

San Andreas's world is about 6 km across and its collision is loaded as static per-sector files, so Skyrim's ground has to be built there at runtime, around CJ, with a **floating origin**.

Two approaches were on the table: spawn collision objects around CJ, or hook San Andreas's collision queries to test against Skyrim's mesh directly. **Spawned collision objects won**, and the query hooks aren't needed:

- San Andreas's collision format takes triangle meshes as they are (`CColModel` with compressed vertices and triangles), so a chunk becomes one invisible `CBuilding` whose model has the chunk as its collision. Every collision query San Andreas makes (peds, cars, camera, line of sight) then sees Skyrim's ground with no hook at all.
- The pools weren't the limit: San Andreas keeps at most 225 chunks (15 x 15), and its building pool has room for them next to the 9,200 map buildings the empty world takes out of the world. The limit that did bite is the store of atomic model infos, which is full (`CModelInfo::AddAtomicModel` crashed), so each chunk slot borrows the model info of one of the map's own atomic models instead, unused once the map is emptied.
- Hooking the queries would have meant reimplementing San Andreas's sphere, box and line tests against a foreign mesh across a dozen functions, for no gain over handing it collision it already understands.

How it works:

- **Skyrim side** (`skse/src/collision/`, ported from PerkeleRim): while San Andreas has the player, the collision sampler reads the land as a heightfield (33 x 33 Havok raycasts per 2048-unit chunk) plus the statics' real collision triangles, nearest chunks first, and streams each as a `CollisionChunk` through the ring ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#collision-chunks)). Chunks are tagged with the handover they belong to.
- **Floating origin** (`protocol/cpp/ground.h`, game-free and tested): a handover puts the base at the Skyrim player's feet, which sits at San Andreas (0, 0, 500 m), high above San Andreas's empty map and its sea. Skyrim position `p` is at `(p - base) / 70 + (0, 0, 500)` metres. Once CJ is 1 km out, or 300 m above or below 500 m, the base moves under him by whole metres, and CJ, the chunks, the camera and the remembered spots all move back by the same shift. Skyrim never notices: poses stay offsets from the handover spot.
- **San Andreas side** (`sa/src/chunk_world.cpp`): each chunk becomes one invisible building positioned at the chunk's middle (its median height), its vertices within 256 m of it (San Andreas packs them as int16 at 1/128 m; triangles out of reach are dropped and counted). San Andreas faces a triangle the other way round from Skyrim, so two corners swap. A resend replaces the chunk in its slot; chunks more than 7 from CJ go, which is always past the 3 at which Skyrim forgets them around the player and a point up to 3 chunks ahead (and resends them when the player comes back). Every triangle is tarmac: chunks carry no material yet. Chunks for a handover San Andreas hasn't read yet wait until it has.
- **Waiting for the ground**: after a handover CJ is held at the handover spot until the chunk under it is in, then let go.
- **Fall rescue** (`protocol/cpp/fall_rescue.h`, PerkeleRim's rule): with no surface under his feet but one above for a quarter second (he slipped through the land or into a rock), CJ goes back where he last stood if that was within 25 m, else onto a surface at most 1 m over his feet, never a ceiling. With nothing above or below at all (a hole), he goes back where he last stood. In a vehicle the vehicle is watched instead, by its middle (the bottom of its collision box hangs below its tyres), standing while a wheel touches.
- **Position guard** (`protocol/cpp/position_guard.h`, game-free and tested): checked every frame before anything else, so a broken position never reaches the origin, the ground or the fall rescue. CJ at a position that isn't a number, more than 100 m under where the origin would have moved (San Andreas's own "fell through the map" rescue drops a player's car at its sea level), or 20 km out, comes out of any vehicle whose own position is broken (one he can't leave goes with him, stopped), goes back where he last was sound, or to the handover's spot when nothing sound is remembered or that spot broke again within 2 s, with his speed, spin and pushes cleared and every broken camera position mended. The floating origin itself ignores a position or an anchor that isn't a number. Every handover puts CJ down from the same clean state.
- **Fast vehicles**: Skyrim samples around a second point up to 3 chunks ahead of the player (2 s of travel), with twice the time per frame above 10 m/s, so the road ahead of a car at 50 m/s is in before it gets there. Skyrim itself may still be loading the land out there, so a chunk that came in incomplete is sampled again after 0.4 s at speed instead of 2 s.

### Running San Andreas hidden

San Andreas runs windowed and minimized, started by `scripts/launch-sa.ps1` and driven from Skyrim. AhShitRim does this in its own ASI rather than with a windowed-mode mod, to keep San Andreas as close to headless as it gets:

- **Window** (`sa/src/window.cpp`): RenderWare opens its windowed video mode, and every Direct3D device is created windowed and without vsync. The window is minimized and never activated (`launch-sa.ps1 -Visible` shows it in a small 800x600 window instead, still without taking the focus, to watch CJ). Losing the focus never reaches San Andreas, which would otherwise pause its loop and sounds and open its menu, and it can't move the cursor.
- **Straight into a game** (`sa/src/runner.cpp`): the splashes, movies and main menu are skipped and a new game loads (about a second, with no map). The mission script never runs; CJ is created where it would have created him, and dressed as its intro dresses him (vest, jeans, sneakers).
- **Empty world**: map collision and IPLs are never streamed in, and every building, dummy and object loaded at start is taken out of the world. No peds, traffic, parked cars, trains, planes or wanted level. CJ stands on Skyrim's ground, built around him (see Ground).
- **Frame rate**: physics stays capped at 30 fps, since higher frame rates break it. While hidden nothing is rendered (San Andreas's `Idle` runs the simulation and returns before drawing), so the only cost is the simulation. Peds' bones are only posed while rendering; CJ's model in Skyrim, which needs his skeleton, will have to pose them itself. Sounds are muted except while CJ has the player or a sound cue plays (see Sound and the radio).
- **Hooks**: functions that only need to be disabled are hooked at their start; the per-frame ones (`Idle`, `FrontendIdle`, `CPad::UpdatePads`) are hooked at their call sites once the window shows, after every other plugin has patched the game, because SilentPatch patches a call inside `Idle`'s first bytes.

F10 in Skyrim hands the player to San Andreas, as in PerkeleRim: Skyrim forwards WASD, mouse look, sprint, jump, crouch, walk, the enter key, the handbrake and the horn, and the vehicle spawn keys (see [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#latest-value-slots) and the README's keys), as actions in the modern layout (Shift sprints, Space jumps, Left Ctrl crouches) or San Andreas's classic PC one (Space sprints, Shift jumps, C crouches; `[Input] KeyLayout` in `AhShitRim.ini`, mapped in `protocol/cpp/input.h`), forces its first-person camera state and moves its player by CJ's offset from the handover spot every frame, looking where San Andreas's camera looks. Once CJ's model is in, Skyrim draws him instead of its player and views him through San Andreas's camera (see CJ in Skyrim). Escape, Tab and the console stay Skyrim's (v20: not M, the map, a letter of the cheat codes; the map is in Tab's menu); a pausing menu pauses San Andreas too. Skyrim's sneak eye is hidden every frame CJ doesn't crouch, since Skyrim kept it up after a crouch (as in PerkeleRim). E activates what CJ faces through Skyrim (see Activating things); talking, shouting and scenes are in Talking, shouting and scenes. The handover ends on F10, when the link goes down or the player dies, or when a game loads; when the game moves the player it goes on, re-anchored (see Load doors, interiors and fast travel).

### Load doors, interiors and fast travel

The game moves the player while CJ has it through a load door, by fast travel or from a script. CJ goes along: the handover goes on with a new anchor where the game put the player.

- **Noticing it** (`protocol/cpp/anchor.h`, game-free and tested): Skyrim says so itself, with a `PositionPlayerEvent` (its `kFinish`) whenever it positions the player for a load door, fast travel, `coc` or a script, and the next frame with a cell re-anchors. Comparing cells alone missed Whiterun's gate out to Tamriel in game: its two sides stand 272 units apart. As a fallback, every frame Skyrim also compares the player's cell and position with where it put the player for CJ the frame before (moving the player for CJ never counts as Skyrim positioning it). A new cell with an interior on either side, or in another worldspace (a city's gate, Tamriel to Whiterun, moves the player only a few metres), is a load door; a jump of over 512 units from where Skyrim put the player for CJ the frame before is fast travel, a script or a load door within one cell (CJ's own movement doesn't count, since the player is put where he is every frame; only Skyrim easing the player out of a wall does, by well under a metre); no cell yet is a loading screen, which waits. Walking into the next exterior cell is nothing. A loading screen is a pausing menu, so San Andreas is frozen meanwhile.
- **Re-anchoring**: Skyrim counts `InputState.handover` up and sends the player's new feet and heading as the anchor, then starts the collision sampler over for that handover, so chunks of the old place are dropped as stale wherever they still are. San Andreas does what it does for every new handover: CJ comes out of his vehicle and every vehicle goes (fast travel in a car leaves the car behind), the old ground is taken out, the floating origin's base is reset exactly to the new anchor (whatever origin moves came before), and CJ is put at San Andreas (0, 0, 500 m) facing the new heading and held there until the chunk under him is in. What San Andreas keeps on CJ himself (his crouch, later his weapons and health) stays. Skyrim keeps its player hidden until CJ is drawn at the new place, a frame or two later.
- **Interiors**: the collision sampler sends an interior's chunks as its statics only (no land, no LOD), complete once every static's 3D is loaded. The chunk grid and the floating origin work the same on an interior's own coordinates, often thousands of units below anything outside.
- `scripts/fake-skyrim.ps1` re-anchors CJ out of a ZR-350 to a spot 60,000 units off and 10,000 below, checks the vehicles go and CJ is held at the new anchor until its ground arrives, then walks him on it.

### Activating things

Skyrim's own activate key refuses while CJ has the player, as all its player controls do, and E activates instead (`skse/src/player/Activation.cpp`, the game-free half in `protocol/cpp/activate.h`).

- **The pick**: a ray from CJ's chest (100 units over his feet, where Skyrim puts the player) along San Andreas's camera direction, as far as Skyrim's `fActivatePickLength` (180 units). Every reference within reach of the player that Skyrim's activate key acts on (doors, activators, containers, people, furniture, flora and everything that can be picked up) is a box: its base object's bounds on its 3D's world transform, a standing box for a living person (their bounds are often empty), or the 3D's bounding sphere for a body on the ground or empty bounds. The nearest box the ray enters wins, except that a living person beats furniture the ray enters before them (a Jarl on his throne: the throne's box is bigger and sticks out in front), if a line of sight ray (Havok, the LOS layer, through the player's own body) meets nothing nearer than it, and it has a name. Skyrim's crosshair shows nothing for a nameless reference, and those (trigger boxes and helpers whose activate text is just "Activate") were often big enough that CJ stood inside them and they won whichever way he looked.
- **The prompt**: Skyrim's own activate text for it (`GetActivateText`, "Open\nBreezehome"), font tags dropped and lines joined, shows in San Andreas's help box top left while CJ faces it ("E: Open Breezehome").
- **Activating**: E calls Skyrim's `ActivateRef` with the player as the activator, so locks, crime, ownership, quest scripts, dialogue, containers and load doors all work as for Skyrim's own key. In a vehicle nothing is picked: load doors only open on foot.

### Talking, shouting and scenes

The game-free rules are in `protocol/cpp/scene.h`; Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/PlayerSync.cpp`, San Andreas's in `sa/src/scenes.cpp` (protocol v9).

- **Talking**: E on a person starts Skyrim's own dialogue. While a menu has the input CJ stands still (the input's axes and buttons are zero), and while the dialogue is open (`MenuTopicManager`, a barter or gift over it too) Skyrim sends the heading from the player to the speaker with `kInputFace`, and San Andreas turns CJ to it at a turn a second. Skyrim also turns its own camera to the speaker on top of the player's angles; that turn is left out by keeping the camera node's offset from the player's angles (`Camera::OffsetOf`) from the last frame without a menu, so San Andreas's camera doesn't swing.
- **Shouting**: Skyrim's Shout handler is the one player control left alone while CJ has the player, so Skyrim's own Shout key (Z by default) shouts as in Skyrim: word charging, the Thu'um, the cooldown and the projectile, from the player, which stands where CJ does and is turned where San Andreas's camera looks every frame. When the player's voice cooldown starts (the shout went off), Skyrim counts `shoutCount` up, and San Andreas plays ped.ifp's `SHOVE_PARTIAL` and `FACANGER` over whatever CJ does on foot, once.
- **CJ's Thu'um** (v25, `protocol/cpp/thuum.h`): CJ shouts in his own words. The Dragonborn's voice isn't part of the shout's spell or its sound (the thunder is the magic effect's release sound, and stays): Skyrim.esm's VoicePowers quest says the words through four dialogue topics (Shout01a, Shout01b, Shout02, Shout03), whose lines are picked by the shout and the speaker's voice type and also serve dragons, draugr and the Greybeards. While CJ has the player, Skyrim (`skse/src/player/Shouting.cpp`) puts a condition in front of each of those lines' own, "the speaker isn't the player" (`GetIsID Player == 0`, ANDed with the rest), and takes it out again when he gives the player back, so everyone else's Thu'um is untouched. While the player charges, the high process's current shout and variation are noted; at the release Skyrim asks CJ for a line of a shout's kind (`lineKind` from `Thuum::kLineShout` up, no new field), which carries the shout (its index in the table of the 27 shouts a player can learn in Skyrim, Dawnguard and Dragonborn, by master file and form ID there) and the words (the variation plus one; by the nearest cooldown if none was seen). San Andreas has CJ say a line for it at once (`SayNow`, as for the insult key): the user's picks for that shout and word count, else the shout's picks for the nearest word count (fewer words first on a tie), else a generic pool of his angry lines (his AG and AR moods' fight lines until picked), each pool a shuffled deck with no repeats back to back. The picks are made in the voice picker's shout columns (`scripts/voice-picker`), one per shout and word count plus the generic pool, and written into `voice_lines.h` by `apply.mjs` as ids only.
- **Swearing** (v16, as in My Summer Car): two keys, each a counter in the input. X (`insultCount`) has CJ say his own generic insult (`CPed::Say` with `CTX_GLOBAL_GENERIC_INSULT_MALE`, which his voice plays as `CTX_PLY_GENERIC_INSULT_MALE`) and, on foot, play ped.ifp's `FUCKU`, the finger `CTaskSimpleShakeFist` plays. The middle mouse button (`swearCount`) has him swear to himself with no gesture (`CTX_GLOBAL_CRASH_GENERIC`, his crash exclamations). Both work in a vehicle too. San Andreas waits a context's `RepeatTime` (7 s, plus up to 1 s) before it plays again and drops a line while CJ still speaks, so each press stops his current line (`CAEPedSpeechAudioEntity::StopCurrentSpeech`) and zeroes the context's next play time (`gGlobalSpeechContextNextPlayTime`) first; its phrase memory still keeps repeats apart. Not while Skyrim moves the player in a scene or while Wasted. The lines themselves are picked by ear: `scripts/voice-picker/extract.mjs` reads CJ's 20 voice banks (690 to 709 in `audio/SFX/SPC_PA`, ten moods each with a second take, through `audio/CONFIG/BankLkup.dat`) from the player's own install into WAVs and a local page under `extracted/voice-picker/` (git ignores it, nothing is shipped), sorted by gta-reversed's table of CJ's speech contexts (`scripts/voice-picker/ply-speech.json`); `apply.mjs` writes the downloaded picks into `protocol/cpp/voice_lines.h`, the conversation categories' picks into a block of their own. AddSayEvent's call of `GetSoundAndBankIDs` (at 0x4E67E2) is redirected, and when a key has picks the next one of a shuffled deck is put in the speech entity's bank and sound instead of San Andreas's choice (by CJ's mood, which in AhShitRim is always CR: no wanted level, no mission just passed). A key with no picks plays San Andreas's own choice.
- **Conversations as cutscenes** (v21; game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/dialogue.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/Conversation.cpp`): while the dialogue menu is open (`MenuTopicManager.menuOpen`, its `speaker`), San Andreas's letterbox bars slide in (`SaHud::SetLetterbox`) and Skyrim's camera leaves San Andreas's for the cutscene's own shots (`PoseCamera` puts the camera node there): a two-shot first (2.5 s), side on to CJ and the speaker, far enough for both faces to sit in the middle of a 60 degree view, then the speaker over CJ's shoulder (45 degrees, from behind his head, looking at the speaker's head node). Every shot of a conversation stays on the side of the two that San Andreas's camera was on when it opened, so they never swap sides on screen, and a LOS ray from what the shot looks at pulls the camera in front of a wall (people don't count). Each topic the player picks cuts back to the two-shot for 2 s. A pick is noticed from `MenuTopicManager` under its lock: a new `lastSelectedDialogue` (or its response), or the same topic picked again, seen as its response (`currentTopicInfo`) starting a second time. CJ answers with a line of the topic's category, sent as `InputState.lineCount` and `lineKind`: greet when the dialogue opens, the topic's when one is picked, goodbye when it closes unless he spoke in the last 2.5 s (a topic that ends the dialogue keeps his answer as the last word). The category comes from `protocol/cpp/dialogue_topics.h`, a table of Skyrim.esm topic FormIDs, else from a keyword rule on the text the player picked (an intimidation threatens, then goodbyes, questions, refusals, greetings, jokes, threats and agreement; anything else asks). The table holds the main quest up to the Greybeards (MQ101 to MQ105) and the generic topics the rule gets wrong, by hand; `protocol/tools/topic-table.mjs` reads Skyrim.esm and its strings (in `Skyrim - Interface.bsa`) from the player's own install and writes only IDs and categories, and with an 8-ball key it classifies every town topic with the homelab's local LLM (`EIGHTBALL_KEY`, `EIGHTBALL_URL`). San Andreas plays the next of a shuffled deck per category (`voice_lines.h`'s dialogue picks) through the same `GetSoundAndBankIDs` redirect as the swear keys, with the insult context as the carrier, also in a scene; without the redirect CJ stays quiet rather than insult anyone. The decks start as CJ's calm (CR, CR2) lines of the San Andreas contexts nearest each category (`GENERIC_HI_MALE`, `AGREE_TO_DO_DRIVEBY` and `SOLICIT_GEN_YES`, `SOLICIT_GEN_NO` and `GANGBANG_NO`, `UH_HUH`, `PULL_GUN`, `CASINO_WIN`, `SHOP_LEAVE`; `apply.mjs --dialogue-defaults`), and the voice picker's "talk" menu picks them by ear.
- **Subtitles**: Skyrim's HUD gets each subtitle, in dialogue or out of it, as a HUD message to show it and one to hide it (`HUDMenu::ProcessMessage`, hooked). While CJ has the player the line is drawn in San Andreas's subtitle style instead (`SaHud::HoldSubtitle`, bottom centre, on the letterbox's bottom bar in a dialogue) whatever Skyrim's own subtitle settings (San Andreas's cutscenes always have them), and taken down the moment Skyrim hides it (`SaHud::HideSubtitle`: a skipped line goes at once). Skyrim's own subtitle clip in its HUD movie is hidden meanwhile. Skyrim's `SubtitleManager` holds only the general chatter's lines, not the dialogue's, and with Skyrim's subtitles off the HUD may get no text, so in a dialogue the response the speaker says now (the current response of the `MenuTopicManager` dialogue leading to `currentTopicInfo`) stands in.
- **Skipping a line**: Skyrim's activate handler skips the speaker's line in a dialogue, so it is let through then (E, as in Skyrim); the left mouse button skips through the dialogue menu itself. Space doesn't skip: neither a press of the activate key sent to the handler by hand nor letting Space through its check did anything.
- **Triggers**: word walls and other trigger boxes see the player, which is where CJ is.
- **Scenes**: each frame Skyrim decides who moves the player. Its sit state (sitting down, seated, standing up; a carriage is a seat too), a bed, movement controls taken away (`DisablePlayerControls`) or a package walking the player (`SetPlayerAIDriven`) hand it to Skyrim. Skyrim then stops putting the player where CJ is and sends the player's feet and heading with `kInputScripted`. While it sits (`kInputSeated`) it sends the seat instead: the furniture marker the player uses (Skyrim's process says which, else the nearest) on the furniture's 3D, where the player's root goes, since its position can stay where it got on. A carriage is the exception: its one marker is where the player gets on, and Skyrim's animation carries the player into the cart from there, so CJ sits on the ground beside it (a known issue). San Andreas takes CJ out of a vehicle (the vehicle stays), turns his collision and gravity off and puts him there every frame, the fall rescue off. A seated player gets `SEAT_IDLE`, which lowers CJ backwards onto a chair behind where he stands (his pelvis about 0.5 m back), and a marker is on the seat's surface, so he is moved by his pelvis's offset from his position (from his posed bones, smoothed) to put it on the seat, 0.12 m over it; otherwise CJ walks or runs on his own move group by the player's smoothed speed, with margins between gaits, and idles below. Skyrim lets its movement and activate handlers through while the player sits, which stands it up. When Skyrim lets go, CJ's collision, gravity and own on-foot task come back where he is, and Skyrim goes back to following him. A re-anchor (a carriage's fast travel) goes on as usual meanwhile.
- **Hiding the player**: Skyrim showed the player's root again while getting into a carriage, so its shapes are culled one by one as well as its roots.

### CJ in Skyrim

CJ's model and animation come from the running San Andreas, like PerkeleRim's models from MSC: nothing is converted, shipped or written to disk.

- **The model** (`sa/src/cj_body.cpp`): CJ's clump, as `CClothes::RebuildPlayer` built it, is one skinned atomic. Its geometry (vertices, normals, UVs, triangles per material), its skin (four bone indices and weights per vertex) and its textures go to Skyrim as one blob in `ModelChunk` messages over the ring back ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#cjs-model)), whenever the link comes up and whenever the geometry changes. Textures are read from their Direct3D 9 textures (the raster's Direct3D part) and decoded to RGBA (`protocol/cpp/texture_decode.h`: DXT1/3/5 and the plain formats). Meshes are split per material and put in Skyrim units (`protocol/cpp/skin_build.h`); a triangle facing against its vertices' normals is turned round.
- **The skeleton**: San Andreas only updates a ped's bones in full when it is on screen, and while hidden nothing is. A `CEntity::UpdateAnim` hook runs CJ's animations as if he were on screen, and after each frame the ASI poses his hierarchy (`UpdateRpHAnim`, then `ShoulderBoneRotation`, which sets the breast bones between clavicle and upper arm and otherwise only runs while drawing; without it the shoulders stretched back) and turns each bone into the transform RenderWare's skin pipeline would use (skin-to-bone, then the bone, then the atomic's frame for a local-space hierarchy; `protocol/cpp/body.h`), relative to his feet, in Skyrim units. With San Andreas's active camera (position, direction, field of view) it goes into the body slot every frame.
- **Drawing** (`skse/src/body/ModelView.cpp`, which draws the vehicles too): the model is built as PerkeleRim's ModelView builds MSC's (BSTriShapes cloned from a vanilla shape, hand-made textures and shaders), but each shape's vertex buffer is dynamic: every Skyrim frame each vertex is skinned on the CPU by its bones (about 1,700 vertices) and written into it. Skyrim's own skinning (`NiSkinInstance`, `NiSkinPartition`) isn't used; it would have to be put together by hand and is far more likely to crash than a rigid shape whose buffer changes. CJ is drawn two-sided (a closed mesh, so it costs nothing visible and the winding can't turn him inside out), lit and taking shadows but casting none yet. A weapon isn't: San Andreas gives each of its faces two sides as two triangles back to back with opposite normals and culls the one turned away, so a mesh of such twins (`SkinModel::OneSided`: at least 80% of its triangles in pairs) is drawn one-sided, or the pair fights over the depth buffer; one with only a few (the parachute's canopy) stays two-sided, or its single faces went missing from behind. He hangs in the node the player's own 3D hangs in, at the handover spot plus his feet; the player's own 3D is hidden meanwhile.
- **Camera**: Skyrim's camera stays in its first-person state (which only draws the player's hidden arms) and its camera node is put where San Andreas's camera is, looking where it looks, with San Andreas's field of view (both are horizontal for a 4:3 screen), as PerkeleRim does for MSC's chase camera. Of the rotation Skyrim built for the node, only its own axes against the player's angles carry over (`Camera::AxesOf`, the nearest signed axis permutation): a camera shake from a hit, or Improved Camera turning the view with the hidden player's head as it staggers, stays out, and San Andreas's own shake comes with its camera. First person isn't supported yet.
- **Smoothing** (`Body::Interpolator`): San Andreas runs at 30 frames a second and Skyrim faster, so Skyrim plays CJ's frames back 50 ms behind, blending bones (normalized quaternion lerp), feet and camera between the two frames around that time. Frames carry San Andreas's high-resolution clock; the gap to Skyrim's clock is the smallest seen, allowed to grow slowly. A new handover starts over.

### Vehicles

San Andreas's own vehicles, physics and all, on Skyrim's ground; Skyrim draws them from San Andreas's own models, the same way as CJ.

- **Spawning** (`sa/src/vehicles.cpp`, the spot rule in `protocol/cpp/vehicle.h`, the kinds in `protocol/cpp/vehicle_kinds.h`): ], [ and `\` in Skyrim ask for a Sandking, a ZR-350 or a Sanchez by model id, and (v19) the dev keys for any other: `;` steps through a list of picks (an NRG-500, a BMX, a quad, a monster truck, the Rhino, the Hunter, the Hydra, two more helicopters, three planes, the jetpack and the parachute, or `[Debug] Vehicles` in `AhShitRim.ini`) and `'` spawns the pick. Player-facing spawning is the cheats'. San Andreas parks it across CJ's path, its side to him, as its own vehicle cheat does, but on Skyrim's ground, which is rarely flat: 15 spots are tried in turn (three rings out from 2 m in front of him, straight ahead, then turned 30 and 60 degrees either way), and the first whose ground is all there under a 3 x 3 grid over the vehicle's footprint, within 0.8 m from highest to lowest (1.2 m for a monster truck, 1.5 m for an aircraft's wider footprint), at most 1.5 m from CJ's feet, and with nothing in the way (from CJ to it, and across it) wins. It is created as a mission vehicle (San Andreas never takes those away), unlocked, a bike on its side stand, and put on its wheels (`PlaceOnRoadProperly`). Every land and air vehicle can spawn (v19): cars, monster trucks, quads, bikes and bicycles, helicopters and planes. Boats can't yet (San Andreas has no water here), nor trains (no tracks), trailers or the radio-controlled ones: Skyrim refuses those itself and says why in the help box, from a table of San Andreas's 212 vehicle models by kind, which San Andreas checks against each model's own type. At most four are kept: another removes the oldest one CJ isn't in. A new handover or a re-anchor takes CJ out of his (`FlushImmediately`, as `WARP_CHAR_FROM_CAR_TO_COORD` does) and removes them all; Skyrim hides them while it has the player.
- **Driving**: San Andreas's own enter and exit tasks, animations and vehicle physics. In a vehicle the keys take San Andreas's vehicle layout on its pad (protocol/cpp/input.h), CJ is seated every frame where his vehicle is (`SetPedPositionInCar`, which also follows a bike's lean), and San Andreas's own rules apply, a rider thrown off a bike on a hard hit among them. One exception: a car in the air (no wheel down) steers half as hard again as San Andreas lets it, up to half as fast again (86 degrees a second about each axis, San Andreas's 57), under San Andreas's own rules (the handbrake makes A/D yaw) except that the gas no longer stops roll and pitch. `Vehicles::Update` adds the pushes San Andreas's capped air control (`protocol/cpp/vehicle.h`) leaves out, for the player's car only, once a frame after San Andreas's physics step (a hook on `CAutomobile::ProcessAI` instead stopped San Andreas taking the keys in that vehicle at all). San Andreas only leans a motorbike in the air (its bicycles yaw), so it also yaws the player's motorbike with A/D, as hard as a car's yaw. The chase camera is San Andreas's own, copied into Skyrim like on foot.
- **The model**: every part (atomic) of the vehicle but the low-detail ones is a bone, its vertices hung on it alone, in the vehicle's own colours (San Andreas swaps the paint and light colours into its materials right before drawing a vehicle, so AhShitRim does the same when reading them). A part's frame may carry a scale or a mirror (wheels), which is baked into its vertices once. Parts that look the same are merged into one mesh, so a vehicle is a few dozen shapes. A part San Andreas doesn't draw now is hidden in Skyrim (v19, `BodyState.hiddenParts`: its vertices collapse onto its joint): a door's or a bonnet's whole version once San Andreas swaps in its damaged one (`SetComponentVisibility`), and of a helicopter's rotor and a prop plane's propeller, which come twice in the model, the standing blades while it spins faster than 0.1 radians a step and the blurred disc otherwise (San Andreas fades between them when it draws them).
- **The pose**: nothing draws a hidden vehicle, and drawing is what turns its wheels: `PreRender` spins, steers and drops the wheels onto their suspension, turns a bike's forks and handlebars, and leans a bike (into its parts, under the upright root: the lean put on the root as well leaned it twice as far as CJ). So each frame San Andreas puts every vehicle's matrix into its clump, runs its `PreRender`, works out a bike's lean matrix afresh for seating CJ, and sends every part's frame in the vehicle's slot. A parked vehicle's parts don't move, and Skyrim skins it again only when they do.
- **Damage** (protocol v18; game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/vehicle_damage.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/VehicleFx.cpp`): San Andreas damages its vehicles (bullets still hit them there, crashes too), but none of its particles are drawn. So each vehicle slot carries the vehicle's health, whether it is wrecked or burning, and where its engine is (the model's engine dummy, or the bonnet), and Skyrim shows them there by San Andreas's own thresholds: smoke from 650 down to 250 (`CVehicle::AddDamagedVehicleParticles`), San Andreas's own (`protocol/cpp/smoke_model.h`: `overheat_car`'s WhiteSmoke and BlackSmoke from `effects.fxp`, its `bullethitsmoke` sprite read at startup from the player's own `models/effectsPC.txd`, drawn in each prim's colour as camera-facing quads through `ModelView`, each sprite on a bone of its own, in sizes and fades a bone can't do itself; each prim at San Andreas's own rate for the smoke's strength, the white up to 60 a second at its middle and the black from 0.3 up to 60 a second, with San Andreas's lives, sizes, fades, rise and spin, carried along at the vehicle's speed); under 250 a vanilla fire, which only scales evenly, so four of them stacked and tapering burn tall and narrow, with the black smoke going on (`CAutomobile::ProcessCarOnFireAndExplode`, which blows the vehicle up after 5 s of it). When the slot turns wrecked, Skyrim sets off a fireball twice a rocket's over it, hurting and throwing its people within 9 m like a rocket's, CJ too unless he sat in it (San Andreas's own blast plays the sound and kills him inside, but doesn't hurt him standing next to it). San Andreas draws a wreck scorched, so the ASI builds the wreck's model again with its colours at a fifth (and without the parts that flew off, such as the wheel `BlowUpCar` knocks away); Skyrim burns it while San Andreas's fire on it lasts. Skyrim's blasts don't damage San Andreas's vehicles yet.
- **Particles** (protocol v26; the prims and rules in `protocol/cpp/fx_model.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/Particles.cpp`): San Andreas runs its vehicles' and the jetpack's effects hidden but draws none of them, so each body slot carries what they come from (`BodyFx`) and Skyrim lets the sprites out itself, by San Andreas's numbers, the way the damage smoke works. A vehicle slot has its exhausts (the exhaust dummy, mirrored for a double exhaust, pointing backwards), the gas pedal and whether it is in first or second gear, the nitro's strength (`NitrousControl`'s, from its `m_fTireTemperature`: half to full with the gas while it burns, fading from half while it charges again) and a helicopter's or plane's rotor dust (`DoHeliDustEffect`'s level, which `PreRender` steps hidden too, and the ground under it, a line down San Andreas's ground); the gear slot has the jetpack's two jets (each jet ball 15 cm down its axis, pointing down it) and its flames' level, which only `RenderJetPack` steps in San Andreas, so the ASI steps it the same way (a tenth a frame towards 1 while CJ thrusts, towards 0 when not). Skyrim then plays San Andreas's own prims from `effects.fxp`: the exhaust smoke as `AddExhaustParticles` puffs it (its chance a frame from the gas pedal, two sprites a puff at each exhaust and one more in a low gear revving, backwards at rest and along at 0.6 of the vehicle's speed when it drives forward, shorter and fainter the faster it goes, drawn as fewer sprites since a model's fade is fixed), the jetpack's orange `Nitrocenter` and blue `gasholding` flames, the nitro's `Cooler`, `Nitro` and `Nitrocenter`, `heli_dust`'s dust, and on the Rhino's shell and the Hunter's gun shots (flags on their events) `tank_fire`'s smoke and flash and `gunflash`. Each prim's keys over its system's time (the level) give its rate, life and speed, its keys over a sprite's life its size, colour and alpha; a sprite is drawn on one of 64 bones of the prim's model, in eight sizes and shades over the part of its life it can be seen in, the newest first. The jetpack's, nitro's and dust's sprites go along with their source (San Andreas's local particles), the smoke on its own. Each sprite comes from the player's own `models/effectsPC.txd`.
- **Aircraft and their guns** (v19): helicopters and planes are San Andreas's own (`CHeli`, `CPlane`), flown with its own controls: W and S throttle, A and D roll, the up and down arrows pitch, Q and E the rudder (L2 and R2), the landing gear on R3 (v23: 2, numpad + or numpad 5, San Andreas's sub-mission key; `CPlane::ProcessControlInputs` toggles it on R3's press in the air, not on the horn as v19 had it), the numpad's 8 and 2 (or Page Up and Page Down) the Hydra's nozzles between hover and forward flight (the right stick). The mouse buttons fire a vehicle's weapons (Circle and L1): the Hunter's guns go to Skyrim as CJ's shots, its rockets and the Hydra's missiles as his projectiles (the vehicle made them), and the Rhino's shell, a line test and an explosion in San Andreas, as a fast rocket without a model from the turret's muzzle towards where San Andreas's line ended, so Skyrim blows it up on whatever is in the way. The Rhino's turret follows San Andreas's camera, as in San Andreas. A vehicle blowing up is Skyrim's fireball of v18, aircraft too.
- **Ground ahead of fast vehicles** (v19): Skyrim samples the chunks around the player and around the point it will be in 2 s, at most 6 chunks (175 m) ahead, a Hydra's 87 m/s, with 6 ms a frame for it over 40 m/s, and San Andreas keeps every chunk within 10 of CJ (`Ground::Ahead`, `kKeepRadius`), so what it drops is always what Skyrim forgot. Rays reach 1 km below the player, so an aircraft high up still finds the land. Each chunk San Andreas keeps fills the collision model of the map model its slot borrows: San Andreas's pool of collision models (10,150) is all but full of the emptied map's own, with room for only about 90 more. Skyrim's own cells only load 2 around the player (`uGridsToLoad` 5), and its land has collision only there, so ahead of a low, fast aircraft the ground can still be missing when it gets there. Measured at a Hydra's 87 m/s (`skse/src/debug/StreamTest.cpp`, `[Debug] StreamTest=87`, from Riverwood heading north-east 30 m up): over land Skyrim held 60 fps with no frame over 50 ms for 3.4 km, the land under the player and 1 s ahead always had collision and 2 s ahead never; past the north coast, over the Sea of Ghosts, it fell to 5 fps. So the Hydra is marked experimental in the README.
- **The jetpack and the parachute** (v19, `sa/src/cj_gear.cpp`, the parachute's rules in `protocol/cpp/parachute.h`): the dev path's model ids 370 and 371 put San Andreas's jetpack on CJ (`CTaskSimpleJetPack`, as its cheat does: its own controls, sprint up, jump down) and give him the parachute (a weapon, worn on his back as v12 draws it). The jetpack is drawn in Skyrim from its own model in the gear slot, placed on his back each frame as `RenderJetPack` places it, its jets burning with San Andreas's flames (v26, Particles above). The skydive is San Andreas's parachute script's, which never runs, so AhShitRim plays it: falling fast carrying the parachute with 15 m of air under him, falling fast or (v20) 3 m below where he last stood (so walking off a bridge or a peak counts, not only bailing out of an aircraft), CJ takes it out and skydives with parachute.ifp's animations (held at full blend over San Andreas's own flailing fall right before his animations update, his face left to his own animations and his idle, since parachute.ifp doesn't animate it), the fire key opens the chute, whose skinned canopy plays its `_O` animations at his back and goes to Skyrim in the gear slot, W and S dive and flare, A and D turn, and on landing the parachute is used up. Skyrim's own fall damage is kept off the player (its fall height reset every frame CJ places it), since San Andreas works out CJ's falls.
- **Floating origin**: the vehicles move back with CJ and the chunks when the origin moves, and with them the points where their wheels last touched the ground, which San Andreas keeps in world space for the next physics step (left behind, they stopped a car at 49 m/s dead and rolled it). `scripts/fake-skyrim.ps1` drives a Sandking 1.3 km, through an origin move, on ground streamed around it.

### Weapons

San Andreas keeps CJ's weapons (its slots and ammo, the weapon in his hands, its animations, aiming and firing); Skyrim only forwards the mouse and draws what it is told (protocol v12; game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/weapons.h`, San Andreas's half in `sa/src/cj_weapons.cpp`, Skyrim's in `skse/src/player/Arms.cpp`, `skse/src/body/MuzzleFlash.cpp` and `skse/src/hud/SaHud.cpp`). What they hit is Skyrim's to say (protocol v13; game-free numbers in `protocol/cpp/damage.h`, San Andreas's half in `sa/src/cj_shots.cpp`, Skyrim's in `skse/src/player/Shots.cpp`).

- **Input**: the left button is Circle (fire), the right one R1 (aim), on foot only. The wheel's steps are counted like the radio's; on foot San Andreas gets each as one press of R2 or L2, the edge its weapon switch reads, one frame down and one up, the next step after that. The mouse still turns San Andreas's camera, and `CCamera::m_bUseMouse3rdPerson` is on so free aim follows it.
- **Firing hidden**: San Andreas's gun task only marks a shot (`m_IsFiringGunRightHandThisFrame`) and `CPedIntelligence::ProcessAfterPreRender`, a step of drawing the ped, fires it (`CWeapon::Fire`, ammo and all). Hidden it never runs, so the ASI calls it once CJ's bones are posed. The minigun's barrels are turned in `PreRender` as well and are turned by hand.
- **The weapon in his hand**: when the held weapon's clump changes the ASI reads its drawn atomics (not the `gunflash` one) as the parts of a model of kind `kSkinModelWeapon`, built the way a vehicle's is (`vehicle.h`'s baking and merging), once per weapon model and kept. Every frame it puts the weapon's frame on his hand bone's matrix as `RenderWeaponPedsForPC` does (a twin pistol's second hand the left bone's, turned over and nudged), reads the parts' world matrices and writes them to the weapon slot of that hand. Skyrim draws them through the same `ModelView` as CJ, smoothed the same way.
- **The loadout**: the pose carries all 13 slots, the held one and its ammo; the co-save holds them (record version 3), and a loaded save, a new game or a San Andreas that restarted gets a `SetLoadout` command over the ring once the link is up. Skyrim keeps what it last saw until San Andreas shows a loadout it sent. Models San Andreas has to have for the weapons are requested and kept in memory.
- **Scopes and crosshairs**: the pose's camera mode says which San Andreas's camera is in. Through the sniper rifle's scope, a rocket sight or the camera CJ isn't drawn (San Andreas's camera sits in his head) and Skyrim draws San Andreas's own scope over the picture: the sniper rifle's crosshair texture four times, mirrored, with the rest of the screen black, hud.txd's rocket sprite, the camera's viewfinder, and hud.txd's four-corner sprite for the first-person rifle and the aim circle. The aim circle is San Andreas's size (`aimRadius`, growing as he fires) and sits where San Andreas's aim ray through its crosshair shows through Skyrim's own camera frustum, so it covers where the shots go whatever field of view Skyrim's camera has. The weapons' textures (icons, the scoped crosshairs) are read at startup from each weapon's own dictionary in `models/gta3.img` (`protocol/cpp/img.h`), never written to disk.
- **HUD**: the weapon's icon replaces the fist, and the ammo line ("rest-clip" or the total, light blue) sits under it as in San Andreas, from the pose.
- **Shots**: San Andreas has no one in its world to hit, so the ASI catches each shot as San Andreas works it out and sends it over the ring: every bullet at the line tests in `CWeapon::FireInstantHit` (its line after San Andreas's own spread, which a minimized San Andreas only works out once its screen's aspect ratio is set) and the first-person rifles' at `CWeapon::DoBulletImpact`, the sniper rifle's from the camera at `CWeapon::FireSniper`, and each melee blow at its hit frame in `CTaskSimpleFight::FightStrike` (where it lands, its radius and damage from `melee.dat`). A shotgun's shot is one line, which Skyrim fans out into 6 or 8 pellets. A bullet's spread is eased in: the first of a burst goes down the crosshair, later ones keep more of San Andreas's spread the longer the button is held. Skyrim ray tests a bullet through its Havok world on the projectiles' collision layer: statics, furniture, trees, the land and water stop it, it passes through the player, the dead and clutter, and the first living actor takes it. Havok only gives the actor's capsule, so the line passing through a sphere round the head bone is a headshot and a low hit a leg. A blow lands on the actors its sphere reaches, once per blow, and the chainsaw keeps cutting; blows stagger by chance (fists a quarter of the time, clubs half), the chainsaw on every cut, so its victim can't fight back.
- **Damage**: `Damage::ToSkyrim` turns San Andreas's damage into Skyrim's: times 0.8 (a pistol kills an early bandit or draugr in 3 to 5 body shots), times 3 on the head and 10 with the sniper and country rifles (a headshot kills), 0.75 on a limb, less above level 6 (down to 0.4, so later the bigger guns matter on top of Skyrim's own health growth), a half for giants and mammoths and a quarter for dragons (the minigun is a dragon weapon, the pistol isn't). Melee does two and a half times `melee.dat`'s damage (San Andreas's peds have far less health than Skyrim's people) and the chainsaw 150 a second. Skyrim's difficulty doesn't scale it. It goes through Skyrim's own hit (`HitData` with the player as the aggressor, the routine the melee hit calls), so kill credit, crime (a chicken in Whiterun), aggro and essential actors are Skyrim's business. Without that routine (another game version) it falls back to `DoDamage`, which takes health and credits the kill but reports no crime.
- **Impacts**: the race's blood where a hit goes in, and the surface's effect and sound where a bullet stops, from vanilla impact sets (the Hunting Bow's for bullets, the Iron Sword's for blades, the Iron Mace's for the rest), spawned as Skyrim's temporary particles at two and a half times their size; a shape with no material of its own gets stone's. No decals yet.
- **Weapon skills**: Skyrim counts the hits per weapon and sends them back as `Hit` commands; San Andreas counts each as its own (`CStats::UpdateStatsWhenWeaponHit`), so poor, gangster and hitman come as in San Andreas, and with them the dual pistols, SMGs and sawn-offs. The pose carries the 11 skill stats and the co-save holds them (record version 4); a loaded save's `SetLoadout` sets them back. A level up shows in the help box ("AK-47 skill: Gangster"), by `weapon.dat`'s thresholds (`Weapons::SkillLevel`).
- **Arm-aimed guns**: the one-handed guns (pistols, micro SMG, Tec-9, sawn-off) are aimed by San Andreas's arm IK (`IKChainManager_c`), which hidden leaves the gun pointing at the sky. So once his bones are posed, while he uses the gun (the aim camera is up, or he fires), the arm is posed as San Andreas's pistol stance looks: upper arm and forearm straight from the shoulder towards San Andreas's aim point (ahead of him when firing without the aim camera), kept on its own side of his body (aiming across him or behind him it stops about 15 degrees past straight ahead and the wrist turns the rest), then the hand turned so the barrel points at the aim point; both arms for twin pistols, before the shoulder bones are set from them. Only the pose: shots go where San Andreas fires them. `AHSHITRIM_AIM_ONLY=1 scripts/fake-weapons.ps1` measures where the one-handed guns point, aiming and from the hip, at each skill level.
- **Explosives and fire** (protocol v15; game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/explosives.h`, San Andreas's half in `sa/src/cj_projectiles.cpp` and `cj_shots.cpp`, Skyrim's in `skse/src/player/Projectiles.cpp`): projectiles fly on the Skyrim side, because that is where the people, the locks, the dragons and the mammoths are; San Andreas's world has only the ground. San Andreas still throws and fires them (its animations, its aim, its force and fuse): `CProjectileInfo::AddProjectile` is caught for CJ, the projectile read as San Andreas made it (where, how fast, which way it faces, its fuse) and sent, then taken back out of San Andreas's world without a blast. Skyrim flies it under San Andreas's gravity with the bullets' ray tests, draws each with San Andreas's model (a second one in flight with a copy of it), and sets it off with Skyrim's own effects: a vanilla fireball explosion for rockets and grenades, with damage through the bullets' hit and table, a knock that ragdolls people (not giants, mammoths or dragons) and locks opened close by; a fireball twice that size and burning oil that spreads over 10 s and burns 30 for a Molotov; a harmless vanilla gas cloud for tear gas, whose choking is ours. Explosives hurt in full whatever the target's level or size. The flamethrower stays a shot: San Andreas sends each puff, Skyrim shoots the Flames spell's flames and burns whoever is in the cone. The spray can and the fire extinguisher are flame shots too: a cone that chokes like tear gas, and one that puts out the burning, the Molotovs' oil and (on San Andreas's side) its own fires and CJ. CJ is hurt by his own as in San Andreas. The sounds, CJ's own burning and the camera shake are San Andreas's: Skyrim's explosions are silent copies, and Skyrim sends each blast, fire and burn back as an Effect, which San Andreas plays with its own routines (its explosion sound and camera shake, its fires, CJ set alight); a rocket and a Molotov keep a harmless stand-in there for the rocket's roar and the bottle's smash. Effects follow one rule: smoke, fog, mist and spray from weapons and vehicles are San Andreas's own particles, read from `models/effects.fxp` and the sprites in the player's `models/effectsPC.txd` and drawn as camera-facing quads through ModelView (the vehicles' smoke in `protocol/cpp/smoke_model.h`, the spray can's and extinguisher's clouds in `protocol/cpp/puff_model.h`, the jetpack's flames, exhaust smoke, nitro, rotor dust and the vehicles' muzzle flashes in `protocol/cpp/fx_model.h`), because Skyrim has no black smoke, green gas or white extinguisher fog and San Andreas's fit far better; explosions and fire stay Skyrim's (a setting for San Andreas's style may come later if players ask). Tear gas still shows Skyrim's gas cloud for now. CJ burning shows Skyrim's flames on him.
- **Satchel charges and the heat seeker** (protocol v17, same files): a satchel goes the same way as a grenade, but sticks where it first touches anything: to an actor's nearest node (a bone of its skeleton), to the 3D of any other reference, or to the land. Its place on that node is kept (`Explosives::StickTo`), so it rides along on a mammoth, a horse or a dragon, and stays where it last was once that 3D unloads. It never goes off by itself. San Andreas keeps a stand-in for each, so it still sees CJ's satchels out and keeps the detonator in his hands (`CWeapon::Fire` gives it after a throw). The detonator (`CWorld::UseDetonator` calling `CProjectileInfo::RemoveDetonatorProjectiles`, hooked) drops the stand-ins and sends a detonation; Skyrim blows every satchel with a grenade's blast, locks included. San Andreas's heat seeker only locks on to vehicles, of which its world has none, so it fires a plain rocket and the lock is Skyrim's: while CJ aims the heat seeker, every dragon, horse (and rider), giant, mammoth and dwarven centurion is rated through Skyrim's camera the way `CWeapon::EvaluateTargetForHeatSeekingMissile` rates vehicles, the best one in sight (a ray every second) is held 1.5 s to lock, and San Andreas's `target256` marker is drawn on it in the HUD, white while locking and red once locked. A rocket fired while locked steers for its target (at most 2.5 radians a second, its speed kept; San Andreas's own homing isn't reversed) and goes off within a metre of its body; one whose target dies or unloads flies on straight. Projectiles are dropped when the player changes interior or worldspace. The Hydra's missiles (protocol v23) go the same way: its own lock (R1 held, `CVehicle::GetPlaneWeaponFiringStatus` 0x6E3440, the heat seeker on L1 once a target has been held 1.5 s) finds no vehicle either, so it fires plain rockets, and Skyrim's lock is aimed through the camera while the handbrake key (R1) is held in the Hydra and steers the rockets launched while it holds (`Explosives::SeeksHeat`); the Hunter's rockets never seek.
- **Muzzle flash**: each shot the pose counts draws three crossed blended quads with a star glow at the muzzle for two San Andreas frames (`flash_model.h`, through `ModelView`) and flashes a Skyrim point light there (`[Weapons] MuzzleLight=0` turns the light off). Sound comes with the audio increment.

### People

Skyrim's people react to CJ's guns and chainsaw as San Andreas's peds do. No plugin file: vanilla AI behaviour, started from the SKSE plugin at runtime (game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/reactions.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/People.cpp`; no protocol change, it reads the pose and the camera San Andreas already sends).

- **Who**: a person (`ActorTypeNPC`) who isn't dead, the player's teammate or commanded (a summon, a thrall), hostile to the player or fighting anyone. So followers keep fighting, bandits fight on, and creatures keep to their own AI. Of the rest, Skyrim's guards (`IsGuard`) are guards (they aren't scared, they comment on the magic) and everyone else a civilian. The Confidence actor value grades civilians: a foolhardy one stands its ground through everything, a brave one doesn't cower at a gun but still runs from gunfire and the saw. Aggression isn't read: an aggressive civilian is already hostile, so it fights.
- **Aimed at** (San Andreas's `CEventGunAimedAt`, hands up and cower): while CJ aims a gun (`kWeaponAiming`), five times a second the nearest person along San Andreas's aim ray (`CCamera::Find3rdPersonCamTargetVector`: from its camera through its crosshair, the middle for a scope), within a body plus 3 degrees of it, past CJ and within 45 m (100 m through a scope), and with a line of sight to the player, is aimed at. A civilian plays vanilla's cowering loop (`IdleCoweringLoose`, event `IdleCowering`) through its AI process (`AIProcess::PlayIdle`) and stands up again (`StopCurrentIdle`) 3 s after CJ stops aiming at it; as it starts it may say a line about the magic (see Guns are magic). One whose behaviour won't take the idle (sitting, at work in furniture) flees instead.

With flying cars on, AhShitRim's extra air control leaves cars to San Andreas's flight model; with infinite ammo San Andreas's rounds don't go down, so the muzzle flash counts the bullets sent instead.
- **Mega punch and floating cars**: IAVENJQ and BSXSGGC are San Andreas's toggles, but San Andreas can't do them here. With mega punch on, San Andreas's blows from fists, brass knuckles and the parachute do the victim's whole health and twenty times the knock, and CJ's blows land on Skyrim's actors, so Skyrim does it (`protocol/cpp/mega_punch.h`, `Shots.cpp`): the blow does four times the victim's health (Legendary quarters the player's damage) through Skyrim's own hit, so an essential actor still kneels, and `AIProcess::KnockExplosion` goes off behind and below the fist, so the victim flies away from CJ and up. Giants, mammoths and dragons, which no blast throws, only die. San Andreas's floating cars (`CPhysical::ApplyCollision`) turn gravity off on a vehicle the player's vehicle hits, but never on a mission vehicle, and every vehicle AhShitRim spawns is one so San Andreas keeps it; so `Vehicles::Update` does it after each step, for the vehicles in the slots among the collision records of CJ's vehicle, with a little lift for a parked one.
- **Their life**: as in San Andreas, cheats don't outlive a load: a loaded save or a new game turns every toggle off. A San Andreas that restarts has them off, so Skyrim sends the ones that are on again when the link comes back.
- **NOMERCY**, ours: nobody is essential but the player. It clears every actor base's essential flag once and every loaded actor's own one every second (a cell loads someone, a quest's alias sets it again), noting each, and typed again gives exactly those back (an actor only while it still has the same base, since a temporary reference's id can be someone else's after a load). It stays on in the save, in its own co-save record; a load gives back the bases this session cleared (they are memory, not the save's) before taking the save's state. Essential actors are what keeps Skyrim's quests whole, so it can break them (the README says so).
- **Ours besides** (v20, until shops and garages bring them): MINIGUN gives the minigun; PAINT resprays and mends the vehicle CJ is in as a Pay 'n' Spray does, and its model is built and sent again in the new colours; LOWRIDER fits San Andreas's hydraulics upgrade to his car, which its own `HydraulicControl` then works with the horn and R3 (numpad 5) and the right stick (the numpad's arrows); and every vehicle comes by its model name, read from the player's own `vehicles.ide`. Ours match by their letters, from 3 long, with the top row's digits. AIR before a plane's name (`protocol/cpp/air_spawn.h`) brings it flying, since Skyrim's rough ground is no runway for the big ones: the typist matches the name as always and looks at the three letters before it, and the spawn request carries `kSpawnAirborne` (`InputState.spawnFlags`). San Andreas puts the plane 150 m over CJ's feet (under the floating origin's 300 m, so a plane never starts over the 800 m where `CVehicle::FlyingControl` cuts its lift, and the ground below is in reach of the rays and the fall rescue), facing the camera's look, gear up (`CPlane::SetGearUp`), engine on, propeller turning, moving forward at 1.3 times the speed at which its form lift holds it level (lift `fFormLift * speed²` against gravity's 0.008 a step: about 50 m/s for the AT-400, 32 for the Dodo), and warps CJ in as its pilot as a save's car does. Helicopters lift off anywhere already, and the Vortex has no lift, so AIR before them changes nothing.
- **Extending it**: a cheat is a row in `kCheats` (its code, kind, San Andreas's index and what it asks for); the wanted level's AEZAKMI will be one.

### Missions

Skyrim's quests start and end like San Andreas's missions, whoever has the player (protocol v24; the game-free rules in `protocol/cpp/missions.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/Missions.cpp`, San Andreas's in `sa/src/audio.cpp`).

- **Which quests**: Skyrim's own rule. Its HUD gets a message when a quest starts, which it only sends once the quest has a journal entry, and one when a quest is completed (`HUD_MESSAGE_TYPE::kQuestStarted`, `kQuestComplete`, with the quest; through the HUD menu's message hook that also takes the subtitles). For a quest that isn't of the Miscellaneous type and has a name, San Andreas's version shows instead and Skyrim's is swallowed (its sound with it). Misc objectives, objectives and failed quests stay Skyrim's.
- **Mission titles**: the quest's name (the HUD message's text, else the quest's) in San Andreas's mission title style, bottom right in gold Pricedown, 4 s and its fades.
- **Mission passed**: San Andreas's `M_PASSR`, "mission passed!" in gold and "respect +" in white under it, for 5 s, over any fade, and San Andreas's mission passed tune as `PLAY_MISSION_PASSED_TUNE 1` plays it: `CAudioEngine::PreloadBeatTrack(11)` (stream track 183, read from the player's own `audio/streams/BEATS`) and `PlayPreloadedBeatTrack(true)`, which stops CJ's radio for it and brings it back after. Nothing is copied: Skyrim sends a sound cue (`InputState.cue`) and San Andreas plays its own.
- **One at a time**: the notices queue (`Missions::Sequence`), each keeping the screen for as long as it shows (a passed mission until its 8.3 s tune is over) and half a second between. A passed mission goes before titles still waiting, since San Andreas ends a mission before the next starts: Before the Storm's end and Bleak Falls Barrow's start come together. A game loading drops what waits.
- **The Helgen intro**: when Ralof's "Hey, you. You're finally awake." in the cart ends (Skyrim's `TESTopicInfoEvent` for MQ101's INFO `00101AEA`, a single response), CJ answers 0.3 s later with "Ah shit, here we go again.": the first line of San Andreas's intro voice-over (the MAIN text `VO_AA`, which its intro script plays as mission audio 43200: SCRIPT bank 353, sound 0, 2.9 s). San Andreas plays it as its scripts do (`PreloadMissionAudio`, `PlayLoadedMissionAudio` once loaded, `ClearMissionAudio` once over), with San Andreas's subtitle for the same 2.9 s. Once until a game loads, so a save from before the cart hears it again.
- **Heard while Skyrim has the player**: San Andreas is muted then, so for a cue's time (its sound plus 1.5 s to load it, `Missions::CueSeconds`) Skyrim sends its volumes as if CJ had the player, and San Andreas reads them while it plays one. Skyrim's own music pauses meanwhile, as for a station, and so do Radio Sovngarde and Sweetroll FM, as San Andreas's radio stops for the tune. Since San Andreas's first read of the input only seeds its counters, Skyrim writes the input once when the link comes up while it has the player, or the first cue of a new game (the Helgen line) would be taken for the seed.
- **Checking it** (`skse/src/debug/MissionTest.cpp`): with `AHSHITRIM_MISSION_TEST=1` in the environment `scripts/restart-games.ps1` runs in, Skyrim's main menu loads the most recent save, CJ's line plays as if Ralof's had just ended, then `resetquest MQ103`, `setstage MQ103 10` and `completequest MQ103` run 10 s apart (or the variable's own `;`-separated console commands); `skyrim` does the same with Skyrim taking the player back first, and `AHSHITRIM_MISSION_SHOTS` names a folder for a screenshot after each command. Nothing is saved. Skyrim's log names every quest HUD message, San Andreas's when each sound loads, plays and ends (checked: the line plays in 31 to 300 ms and ends after 2.9 s, the tune plays for 9.3 s, both while CJ has the player and while Skyrim has it).

### Pickups

San Andreas's spinning pickups float in Skyrim's world (protocol v27). Skyrim keeps them, as it keeps the projectiles; San Andreas lends its models and its sounds. The game-free rules and the spot table are in `protocol/cpp/pickups.h`, Skyrim's half in `skse/src/player/WorldPickups.cpp`, San Andreas's in `sa/src/cj_pickups.cpp`.

- **The spots**: a table in code (no plugin, no ESP), in Skyrim world coordinates read from `Skyrim.esm`: most stand on a location's map marker (where fast travel puts the player). Parachutes on high spots (the Throat of the World's summit, High Hrothgar's terrace, the top of Bleak Falls Barrow, the roof of Dragonsreach in Whiterun's own worldspace, Arcwind Point, Forelhost, the Shrine of Azura, the College of Winterhold, Mount Anthor, Darklight Tower), armour at two or three spots in every hold, hearts in a few villages, a few weapons, and one-offs (a rocket launcher, a minigun and three stashes of gold). Within 100 m of CJ each spot is set down on whatever is under it (a Havok ray from 3 m over its height, a roof's from 60 m, through actors and clutter), floats 0.6 m over it and turns once every 2 s.
- **The models**: the first time a kind is near, Skyrim asks San Andreas for it (a `PickupCommand`); San Andreas loads San Andreas's own pickup model (`health`, `bodyarmour`, `gun_para`, `money`, `jetpack`, a weapon's from `weapon.dat`), builds it as a held weapon's and sends it with its parts' places (a `PickupEvent`). Skyrim draws a copy of it for each one shown at once (`ModelView::AddCopy`).
- **Taking one**: as San Andreas's `CPickup::Update`: on foot, within the square root of 1.8 m across and 2 m up or down of his middle, and not what he can't use (a heart at full health, armour at full armour, the jetpack while he wears one). A heart fills Skyrim's health, armour CJ's armour, money is Skyrim's gold, a weapon comes with San Andreas's street ammo (`AmmoForWeapon_OnStreet`, a `GiveWeapon` command), the parachute goes in his hands and the jetpack on him (`CjGear::Give`); San Andreas plays its pickup sound for each.
- **Coming back**: 30 s after it was taken (San Andreas's `PICKUP_ON_STREET`), armour after 6 minutes, and only once CJ is 10 m off; the clock runs while San Andreas has the player. One-offs never come back, and the co-save remembers them by their spot's id.
- **The heart**: when the player is down to a quarter of its health, a heart turns up in front of CJ, 3 to 8 m off where the camera sees it, at most once every 3 minutes; it goes after 2 minutes or once he is 60 m away.
- **San Andreas's own**: the jetpack CJ takes off (`CTaskSimpleJetPack::DropJetPack` makes a pickup of it) is handed to Skyrim and taken out of San Andreas's list, so it lies where it fell, drawn the same way, until he walks back into it (after stepping off it once) or leaves that place.

### San Andreas's HUD

While CJ has the player, Skyrim draws San Andreas's HUD and text over its own frame, in San Andreas's own fonts and sprites.

- **Files** (`skse/src/hud/SaHud.cpp`): at startup the SKSE plugin reads `data/fonts.dat`, `models/fonts.txd` and `models/hud.txd` from the San Andreas install named in `Data/SKSE/Plugins/AhShitRim.ini` (`[SanAndreas] Path=`, written by `scripts/deploy-local.ps1` from `SaPath`), into memory only. The texture dictionaries are read by `protocol/cpp/txd.h` (RenderWare chunks, Direct3D 8 and 9 native textures, decoded by `texture_decode.h`); without them the HUD stays off and the log says why.
- **Renderer** (`skse/src/hud/Overlay.cpp`): a hook on Skyrim's swap chain `Present` draws a list of textured quads with DirectXTK's `SpriteBatch` (already linked through CommonLibSSE-NG), saving and restoring the Direct3D state it touches, since Skyrim caches its own. PerkeleRim has no overlay (its HUD is a layer in Skyrim's Scaleform HUD movie), and Scaleform can't take bitmap fonts from memory, so this is new here. The main thread builds the quads each frame; Present draws the latest, and nothing once they are a quarter second old, so a pausing menu doesn't keep a stale HUD up. F11 has the next Present save the finished back buffer (quads, Skyrim's HUD and menus) as a PNG with DirectXTK's `SaveWICTextureToFile`, since Skyrim's own screenshot is taken before any of them are drawn, into `AhShitRim.ini`'s `[Screenshots] Dir` (the launcher's `Screenshots` folder) or `Documents\My Games\AhShitRim\Screenshots`, as PerkeleRim keeps its own; the help box then says "Screenshot saved".
- **Fonts** (`protocol/cpp/hud_font.h`): San Andreas's `CFont` rules from gta-reversed: two 512 x 512 textures of 16 x 12.8 cells (Gothic and Menu in `font2`, Subtitles and Pricedown in `font1`, Menu and Pricedown as subfonts further down), letter widths from `fonts.dat` (proportional or fixed, widened by the outline), 32 x 20 font units per letter, 18 per line, `~n~` line breaks, word wrapping, and outlines drawn as eight black copies one edge out.
- **Layout** (`protocol/cpp/hud_layout.h`): San Andreas lays its HUD out on a 640 x 448 screen stretched over 4:3. Here the units are those of a 4:3 screen as high as Skyrim's (square pixels), and each piece is held to the edge San Andreas holds it to, so 16:9, 16:10 and 21:9 show the HUD unstretched in the corners. Top right as `CHud::DrawPlayerInfo`: the weapon icon (the fist until weapons exist), the clock (Skyrim's game time), the armour bar (CJ's armour, hidden at 0; nothing gives him any yet), the health bar (the Skyrim player's health over its maximum, flashing under a tenth) and the money (the player's gold, green, or red when negative), then the wanted stars, shown once there is a wanted level.
- **Text styles**: the mission title (bottom right in gold Pricedown, as San Andreas puts it), big messages in the middle ("Wasted" and "Busted" in grey Gothic, going black with the fade, "mission passed!" in gold Pricedown with "respect +" in white under it, over it), the help box (top left, dark box), subtitles (bottom centre, lines going up, near white with a drop shadow 2 units down and right and no outline, shown and dropped at once with no fades, as San Andreas's screenshots show them; font1's letters are about 87% white, so they are drawn white to come out at San Andreas's 225), fade to black and letterbox bars sliding in. `SaHud.h` is the interface later increments use; `/` (dev key) shows each style in turn.
- **Skyrim's own HUD**: its compass, attribute bars and crosshair (clips of its HUD movie) are hidden every frame CJ has the player, as Skyrim's HUD modes show them again, and put back as they were on F10.

### Sound and the radio

San Andreas plays its own sounds, with its own audio engine; Skyrim only sets how loud.

- **Unmuted while handed over** (`sa/src/audio.cpp`): San Andreas's audio keeps running hidden (`CAudioEngine::Service` runs in `Idle` before its render gate), and every sound buffer it makes already has DirectSound's global focus, so it plays while Skyrim has the focus. Its effects and music master volumes (0..64) are set every frame from the audio slot: Skyrim's master x effects and master x music sliders (`skse/src/audio/SaAudio.cpp`; the effects category is Skyrim.esm's `000172A1`, music comes from the default objects, master is the `fAudioMasterVolume` preference, as it has no category), and 0 while Skyrim has the player. Sounds are positioned by San Andreas's listener, its camera, which Skyrim's camera follows. A pausing Skyrim menu pauses every channel (`CAudioEngine::PauseAllSounds`), on top of San Andreas's user pause.
- **Sound cues** (v24, see Missions): the mission passed tune and CJ's intro line, which Skyrim asks for through `InputState.cueCount` and `cue` and San Andreas plays with its own routines, heard whoever has the player.
- **No world ambience**: `CAEWeatherAudioEntity::Service` (city noise, wind, rain), `CAEAmbienceTrackManager::Service` (area ambience tracks), `CAEPoliceScannerAudioEntity::Service` and `CAEGlobalWeaponAudioEntity::ServiceAmbientGunFire` do nothing. CJ's, his vehicles' and his weapons' sounds and his speech are untouched.
- **The radio**: San Andreas starts a vehicle's own station when CJ gets in, as usual. Skyrim's mouse wheel counts steps in the input (`radioNext`, `radioPrev`); in a vehicle San Andreas turns them into `CAudioEngine::RetuneRadio` along the dial 1..14 (`protocol/cpp/radio.h`), and reports the station in CJ's pose, the one the wheel is going to while it retunes (at most 3 s). San Andreas draws the station's name only while rendering, so Skyrim draws it (`SaHud::ShowRadioStation`, `CAERadioTrackManager::DisplayRadioStationName`'s style: Menu font, top centre, gold, dark grey while retuning). Skyrim's own music pauses while any station plays: every frame its current music type gets a `BSMusicEvent` pause (Skyrim moves on to combat or a dungeon's music on its own), and the one paused goes on after. Attenuating the music category didn't reach the tracks.
- **Radio Sovngarde**: the dial's 12 and 13 are the stations Skyrim plays. San Andreas tunes its radio off (its `eRadioID` 13; its User Track Player turns into off too when the player has no tracks) and reports the dial's value in the pose. On 12, Radio Sovngarde, Skyrim plays its own music by path from its archives (`BSAudioManager::GetSoundHandleByFile`, nothing copied): the main theme first, then by mood, combat tracks while the player is in combat and the time of day's exploration tracks otherwise, with one of the 13 bard instrumentals after every two (a mood change cuts the track short with a fade). The bards' sung songs are voice files split by verse, which can't be played from a path.
- **Sweetroll FM**: on 13 Skyrim plays AhShitRim's own CC0 tracks (`music/CREDITS.md`, picked from pages that state CC0) the same way, from loose files in `Data\Music\AhShitRim\SweetrollFM\` (`kSweetrollTracks` in `radio.h`). No mood: a shuffle plays every track once before any repeats and never one twice in a row, starting on a random one when tuned in. At data load Skyrim checks which tracks are installed and plays only those; with none (a dev build before `scripts/encode-music.ps1` ran) tuning in shows the name, stays silent and logs it once. `scripts/deploy-local.ps1` copies `music/sweetrollfm/*.xwm` and the credits there, and the release package ships them in the same place. Skyrim plays only `.wav` and `.xwm`, so the script encodes the sources (mp3, ogg) with ffmpeg and the DirectX SDK's `xWMAEncode`.

## 6. Risks

- **Collision at runtime**: spawned collision objects carry CJ on Skyrim's real terrain and statics, and the vehicles at full speed from Whiterun to Riverwood and past two floating origin moves (both checked in game); `scripts/fake-skyrim.ps1` covers the same on flat test ground. Skyrim's land ahead of a very fast vehicle can still be loading when it is sampled (retried quickly at speed).
- **San Andreas updates** through Steam undo the downgrade. Keep the downgraded copy outside Steam, or run the downgrader again.
- **Two engines in one session**: both games must run at once; San Andreas is light, but its frame pacing affects physics.
